Top 10 TodayOct. 11, 2026
  1. 1
    DOJ Probes TV Networks Over Trump Pool Boycott ↗
    NBC News

    The Justice Department's Antitrust Division is investigating ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC and Fox News after they briefly halted White House TV pool coverage last month when CNN was barred. Civil investigative demands went out Friday; DOJ says group boycotts can violate the Sherman Act.

  2. 2
    House Republicans Move to Block Russia Diesel Deal ↗
    RFE/RL

    Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick plans a discharge petition for the Ronald Reagan Peace Through Strength Act to bar U.S. purchases of Russian oil, with Rep. Don Bacon as co-sponsor. Trump separately urged Ukraine to replace Zelenskyy after Kyiv called the diesel deal a weak decision.

  3. 3
    Philadelphia Officer Shane Seiber Killed in Ambush ↗
    NBC Philadelphia

    Officer Shane Seiber, 40, a 10-year veteran and father of two, was shot three times and died Saturday after a shotgun ambush in West Philadelphia. Suspect Eric Lewis, a city water employee, faces murder and related charges; a second officer shot 12 times is stable.

  4. 4
    US Forces Disable Ship Running Iran Blockade ↗
    Reuters / gCaptain

    CENTCOM said a U.S. fighter jet struck the stern of Panama-flagged M/V Ocean Molica in the Gulf of Oman on Oct. 10 after it ignored warnings and tried to run the naval blockade of Iran. Propulsion was disabled; the crew was not harmed.

  5. 5
    Hundreds of Thousands Still Dark After Isaias ↗
    WSFA / AP local

    As of Saturday night more than 100,000 Alabama Power customers and nearly 300,000 in Florida remained without power after Isaias. Governors reported two deaths in each state; FPL aims to restore 95% of safely restorable customers by Monday night.

  6. 6
    National Guard Photographed Near DC Ballot Drop Box ↗
    NBC4 Washington

    Photos showed armed National Guard members near a Foggy Bottom ballot drop box, drawing concern from D.C. officials. The Board of Elections asked the Guard to stay clear of drop boxes and voting sites; the Guard said members are directed not to monitor polling places.

  7. 7
    Bomb Threats Lock Down Two Brooklyn Jewish Schools ↗
    News 12

    Phone threats minutes apart Friday hit Yeshivah of Flatbush and United Talmudical Academy in Williamsburg. Both schools were evacuated; NYPD Emergency Service and K-9 units found no explosives. Students returned; no injuries or arrests, investigation ongoing.

  8. 8
    ABC News Corrects Deleted Trump Sons Contracts Story ↗
    The Wrap

    ABC removed its Oct. 7 report on Trump sons' investments and Pentagon contracts within a day, then republished a corrected version Friday. An editor's note cited errors on investments, contracts and chronology; Anduril's $2.5 billion round was wrongly tied to 1789 Capital's $50 million stake.

  9. 9
    Admin Explores Ships for Migrant Deportations ↗
    The New York Times

    Officials are weighing ferries and the retired Training Ship Golden Bear for multi-day deportations to Latin America, according to people familiar with talks. DHS called the idea hypothetical with no decision made; journeys could cost millions and raise safety and jurisdiction questions.

  10. 10
    Economic Pain Puts GOP Midterms on Defense ↗
    The New York Times

    Three weeks from Election Day, high prices and Trump's high-30s approval are expanding the map of competitive races into once-safe Republican territory. GOP groups are shifting ad dollars, including another $30 million into Texas for Ken Paxton's Senate seat.

Immigration BriefingOct. 10, 2026
  1. 1
    Judge Blocks Deportation of Man ICE Shot ↗
    ABC7 New York

    A federal judge on Friday barred the government from deporting Oscar Belgal, 28, shot by an ICE agent in Marble Hill with his 5-year-old son in the car. DHS Secretary Mullin said agents feared for their lives; Gov. Hochul called for a pause in ICE actions in New York.

  2. 2
    Labor Dept Subpoenas Nine Universities Over J-1 Visas ↗
    Inside Higher Ed

    VP JD Vance said the Labor Department is investigating Harvard, Yale, Stanford, MIT, Brown, Caltech, Arizona State, UC Davis and Pitt over alleged J-1 visa abuse, and subpoenas have been served. Several schools said they comply with the law and will respond.

  3. 3
    H-1B Workers Weigh Fallout of PERM Green Card Freeze ↗
    Business Standard

    After DOL suspended PERM green card processing for TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Cognizant and three others, lawyers say H-1B petitions were not banned, but extensions and transfers at those firms may face more scrutiny.

  4. 4
    Judge Restores Biometrics for Detained Immigrants ↗
    Financial Express

    Judge Amir Ali blocked a USCIS policy that stopped collecting fingerprints and photos from detained immigrants with pending applications, restoring the earlier process nationwide. It does not grant status or release anyone.

  5. 5
    Court Rules Remain in Mexico Policy Unlawful ↗
    The Independent

    A federal judge found the Remain in Mexico policy illegally blocked asylum seekers' access to protection, counsel and fair hearings; the ruling was unsealed Friday. Advocates say only 7 percent of people subjected to it found lawyers.

  6. 6
    Immigration Board Limits Asylum From 40 Democracies ↗
    DOJ / Board of Immigration Appeals

    In Matter of B-S-A-N-, issued Oct. 9, the Board of Immigration Appeals told immigration judges to generally deny asylum claims from nationals of 40 developed democracies, including EU states, Canada and the U.K., absent extraordinary circumstances.

  7. 7
    Judge Says ICE Agents Falsified Records in Bronx ↗
    Gothamist

    Judge Gary Brown, a Trump appointee, ruled ICE agents illegally arrested two men in the Bronx in May, including a U.S. citizen, then gave false testimony and falsified records. ICE has 21 days to say how it will keep future arrests lawful.

  8. 8
    Medicaid Cuts Drop Refugees and Legal Immigrants ↗
    AP / ABC News

    Changes effective Oct. 1 cut Medicaid for certain legal immigrants, including refugees, asylum seekers and trafficking victims; green card holders were not affected. KFF estimates over 281,000 immigrants in nine states and D.C. lose coverage.

  9. 9
    ICE Finalizes Civil Fines Rule After Court Block ↗
    Hindustan Times

    ICE finalized a rule on civil immigration penalties, Bloomberg Law reported Oct. 9, days after a Massachusetts judge stayed the earlier interim rule. The final rule keeps the framework but allows some late appeals when mail delays prevent timely responses.

  10. 10
    DHS Silent on ICE Pause During Hurricane Isaias ↗
    AP

    DHS would not tell AP on Friday whether non-criminal immigration enforcement will be suspended in states hit by Hurricane Isaias. Past administrations, including before Hurricane Irma in 2017, said such operations would pause in affected areas.
