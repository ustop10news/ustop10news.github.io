Top 10 TodayOct. 13, 2026
  1. 1
    Federal Agents Search Rep. Omar's Home ↗
    AFP

    Homeland Security Investigations agents executed a judicially authorized search of Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington home Monday as part of an ongoing federal criminal investigation. Omar called it political intimidation ahead of the Nov. 3 midterms; no charges were announced.

  2. 2
    Trump Defends Russia Diesel Deal ↗
    RFE/RL

    President Trump on Monday defended allowing Russian diesel imports and insisted an energy cease-fire with Ukraine is in place, even as Kyiv reported further strikes. Bipartisan senators and GOP Sen. Jerry Moran urged a reverse course; Treasury's OFAC license runs through April 2027.

  3. 3
    Taxpayer Ads Aired After Judge Said Stop ↗
    NPR

    NPR found nearly $22 million in taxpayer funds aired DHS-linked Trump promo ads since late September, including about $10 million after Trump pledged MAGA Inc. would pay. Spots kept running for hours after a Sunday night TRO ordered them canceled; the White House blamed late notice.

  4. 4
    Analysts Doubt Diesel Deal Cuts Prices ↗
    CNBC

    Energy analysts said Trump's pledge of 300,000 metric tons of Russian diesel — with more later — is unlikely to dent U.S. prices before the midterms. The U.S. uses about 3.7 million barrels of diesel a day; diesel hit a record average of $6.53 a gallon in late September.

  5. 5
    FPL Restores Power for 85% After Isaias ↗
    FPL

    Florida Power & Light said it restored more than 85% of Isaias outages by Monday night — over 380,000 customers — with restoration essentially complete in six of eight counties. Escambia and Santa Rosa remain hardest hit; FPL aims to finish there by Tuesday night.

  6. 6
    Pennsylvania Reports Sixth Measles Death ↗
    USA TODAY

    A Franklin County coroner said a 39-year-old unvaccinated woman met criteria for a measles-associated death, Pennsylvania's sixth this year. Johns Hopkins tallies 4,267 U.S. cases in 2026; CDC lists 4,080 and two deaths under a stricter new counting method.

  7. 7
    Judge Blocks DOJ Voter-Roll Checks ↗
    Courthouse News

    U.S. District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan vacated the DOJ's policy of cross-checking state voter rolls with DHS's SAVE citizenship database, calling it an attempt to decide who gets to vote. Common Cause plaintiffs included citizens wrongly flagged as noncitizens; DOJ is reviewing the ruling.

  8. 8
    DOJ Probes TV Networks Over Pool Boycott ↗
    NBC News

    The Justice Department sent civil investigative demands to ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC and Fox News over their brief joint pause of White House TV pool duties after CNN was barred from the grounds. DOJ said group boycotts among competitors can violate the Sherman Act; networks and press advocates called it intimidation.

  9. 9
    Trump Heads to Alaska for Close Senate Race ↗
    AP News

    President Trump is traveling to Alaska to stump for Sen. Dan Sullivan in a unexpectedly tight race against Democrat Mary Peltola ahead of Nov. 3. Polling averages have shown Peltola competitive; Vice President JD Vance already campaigned in Fairbanks for Sullivan.

  10. 10
    Buffalo Hate Crimes Trial Jury Selection ↗
    AP News

    Jury selection begins Tuesday in the federal hate-crimes death-penalty trial of Payton Gendron for the 2022 Tops Friendly Market massacre in Buffalo. Gendron already received a life sentence in state court; the federal case carries a possible death sentence.

Immigration BriefingOct. 13, 2026
  1. 1
    DHS Proposes Ending H-1B 'Prior Deference' Policy ↗
    Bloomberg Law

    A DHS proposal released Tuesday would end deference to earlier approvals for H-1B and other temporary workers, adding scrutiny to extension requests even when the job hasn't changed. It revives a first-term Trump policy; Biden restored deference in 2021 and codified it in 2024.

  2. 2
    DHS Finalizes End of Work Permit Auto-Extensions ↗
    Bloomberg Law

    A DHS final rule makes permanent the end of the 540-day automatic extension for work permits pending renewal, affecting asylum seekers and H-1B spouses. It takes effect when published Oct. 14. Over 432,000 renewals had been pending six months as of June 30.

  3. 3
    Federal Agents Search Rep. Ilhan Omar's DC Home ↗
    Reuters via The Star

    Homeland Security Investigations executed a search warrant Monday at Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington home in an ongoing probe, the Justice Department said. A source told Reuters it concerns alleged immigration fraud. Omar called it political intimidation.

  4. 4
    Trump Appeals Second Court Loss on H-1B Fee ↗
    VisaVerge

    The administration asked the Ninth Circuit on Oct. 9 to overturn a California order blocking the $100,000 H-1B fee and to pause it meanwhile. The fee stays blocked under two court orders. A separate $103,265 DHS fee proposal is not in effect.

  5. 5
    ICE Admits Instant Re-Arrests After Court-Ordered Releases ↗
    MPR News

    MPR News found several people re-arrested by ICE at Minneapolis's Whipple building right after judges ordered their release. An ICE official told a lawyer, "we've been doing this since May." DHS and ICE did not respond to questions.

  6. 6
    Medicaid Cuts Drop Refugees, Other Legal Immigrants ↗
    AP via Los Angeles Times

    Since Oct. 1, refugees, asylum seekers and trafficking victims have lost Medicaid under the 2025 tax and policy law; green-card holders weren't affected. KFF estimates over 281,000 immigrants in nine states and DC lose coverage.

  7. 7
    USCIS Inflation Fee Increases Start Oct. 16 ↗
    USCIS

    Inflation-adjusted H.R. 1 fees apply to filings postmarked from Oct. 16; filings without the new fee will be rejected. The annual asylum fee rises to $105, the initial asylum, parole and TPS work permit fee to $570, and the TPS application to $520.

  8. 8
    Study: Nearly 15,000 Deported From Southern California ↗
    ABC7 Los Angeles

    A UCLA and UC Berkeley Deportation Data Project study found nearly 15,000 people were arrested and deported in Southern California from January 2025 to August 2026, mostly via raids, courthouse arrests and check-ins. DHS denies ramping up enforcement.

  9. 9
    Judge Pauses DHS Halt on Detainee Biometrics ↗
    Law&Crime

    U.S. District Judge Amir Ali stayed a DHS policy that stopped collecting fingerprints and photos from detainees with pending USCIS applications, finding it likely violates the APA. The government must show compliance by Oct. 21.

  10. 10
    Judge Halts Trump TV Ads Paid With Border Funds ↗
    Courthouse News

    U.S. District Judge Jennifer Rochon ordered a stop to taxpayer-funded TV ads, finding DHS likely used money appropriated for border security for "publicity or propaganda purposes." The administration had already said it would fund the ads privately.
