  1. 1
    DOJ Probes TV Networks Over Trump Pool Boycott ↗
    NBC News

    The Justice Department's Antitrust Division is investigating ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC and Fox News after they briefly halted White House TV pool coverage last month when CNN was barred. Civil investigative demands went out Friday; DOJ says group boycotts can violate the Sherman Act.

  2. 2
    House Republicans Move to Block Russia Diesel Deal ↗
    RFE/RL

    Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick plans a discharge petition for the Ronald Reagan Peace Through Strength Act to bar U.S. purchases of Russian oil, with Rep. Don Bacon as co-sponsor. Trump separately urged Ukraine to replace Zelenskyy after Kyiv called the diesel deal a weak decision.

  3. 3
    Philadelphia Officer Shane Seiber Killed in Ambush ↗
    NBC Philadelphia

    Officer Shane Seiber, 40, a 10-year veteran and father of two, was shot three times and died Saturday after a shotgun ambush in West Philadelphia. Suspect Eric Lewis, a city water employee, faces murder and related charges; a second officer shot 12 times is stable.

  4. 4
    US Forces Disable Ship Running Iran Blockade ↗
    Reuters / gCaptain

    CENTCOM said a U.S. fighter jet struck the stern of Panama-flagged M/V Ocean Molica in the Gulf of Oman on Oct. 10 after it ignored warnings and tried to run the naval blockade of Iran. Propulsion was disabled; the crew was not harmed.

  5. 5
    Hundreds of Thousands Still Dark After Isaias ↗
    WSFA / AP local

    As of Saturday night more than 100,000 Alabama Power customers and nearly 300,000 in Florida remained without power after Isaias. Governors reported two deaths in each state; FPL aims to restore 95% of safely restorable customers by Monday night.

  6. 6
    National Guard Photographed Near DC Ballot Drop Box ↗
    NBC4 Washington

    Photos showed armed National Guard members near a Foggy Bottom ballot drop box, drawing concern from D.C. officials. The Board of Elections asked the Guard to stay clear of drop boxes and voting sites; the Guard said members are directed not to monitor polling places.

  7. 7
    Bomb Threats Lock Down Two Brooklyn Jewish Schools ↗
    News 12

    Phone threats minutes apart Friday hit Yeshivah of Flatbush and United Talmudical Academy in Williamsburg. Both schools were evacuated; NYPD Emergency Service and K-9 units found no explosives. Students returned; no injuries or arrests, investigation ongoing.

  8. 8
    ABC News Corrects Deleted Trump Sons Contracts Story ↗
    The Wrap

    ABC removed its Oct. 7 report on Trump sons' investments and Pentagon contracts within a day, then republished a corrected version Friday. An editor's note cited errors on investments, contracts and chronology; Anduril's $2.5 billion round was wrongly tied to 1789 Capital's $50 million stake.

  9. 9
    Admin Explores Ships for Migrant Deportations ↗
    The New York Times

    Officials are weighing ferries and the retired Training Ship Golden Bear for multi-day deportations to Latin America, according to people familiar with talks. DHS called the idea hypothetical with no decision made; journeys could cost millions and raise safety and jurisdiction questions.

  10. 10
    Economic Pain Puts GOP Midterms on Defense ↗
    The New York Times

    Three weeks from Election Day, high prices and Trump's high-30s approval are expanding the map of competitive races into once-safe Republican territory. GOP groups are shifting ad dollars, including another $30 million into Texas for Ken Paxton's Senate seat.

Immigration BriefingOct. 11, 2026
  1. 1
    ICE SUV Injures Man in Houston Chase ↗
    Texas Public Radio / Houston Public Media

    On Oct. 10, an ICE-operated SUV struck Miguel Parra-Hernandez, 48, during a Clay Road pursuit near Houston; he was hospitalized with a broken leg and remains in ICE custody. ICE said he ran in front of the vehicle; Harris County deputies are investigating, and the targeted driver remained at large.

  2. 2
    DOL Freezes PERM for Microsoft and IT Giants ↗
    JD Supra / Robinson Immigration Law

    On Oct. 8, DOL announced it will not accept or process PERM green-card labor certifications for Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini. Officials alleged program misuse and cited investigations; existing H-1B status was not revoked, and no end date was announced.

  3. 3
    Coalition Sues USCIS Over Green Card Rules ↗
    SG Legal Group

    On Oct. 5, AAUP and partners sued in Massachusetts over two USCIS policies that tighten in-country green-card adjustments and allow denials without an RFE. Both policies remain in effect pending a court order; the case is AAUP v. Mullin.

  4. 4
    Admin Weighs Ships for Mass Deportations ↗
    VisaVerge (NYT-sourced)

    Officials discussed using vessels including the retired Training Ship Golden Bear and ferries for multi-day deportations to Central and South America. DHS called the idea hypothetical with no decisions made; trips could cost millions and raise safety and legal questions.

  5. 5
    BIA Limits Asylum From Developed Democracies ↗
    Law Office of Alena Shautsova

    In Matter of B-S-A-N- (Oct. 9), the Board of Immigration Appeals told judges to generally deny asylum from nationals of highly developed democracies, with a separate presumption against discretionary asylum for EU citizens, absent extraordinary circumstances.

  6. 6
    Howard County Launches ICE Activity Report Form ↗
    Howard County Government

    On Oct. 9, County Executive Calvin Ball unveiled an anonymous online form for residents and employees to report observed ICE activity, including on county property. Officials said it is for informational tracking, not emergency response.

  7. 7
    Judge Restores Biometrics for Detained Applicants ↗
    Newsweek

    On Oct. 7, Judge Amir Ali stayed a DHS policy that stopped collecting fingerprints and photos from detained people with pending USCIS applications. The order restores the prior process while the suit continues; it does not grant status or release anyone.

  8. 8
    Newsweek: ICE Detained 65 Faith Leaders ↗
    Newsweek

    A Newsweek analysis published Oct. 11 found at least 65 pastors, priests, imams, rabbis and other faith leaders detained by ICE since January 2025; 30 were released, 18 deported, and 14 remain held. DHS said enforcement targets unlawful presence, not faith.

  9. 9
    Legal Immigration Cuts Outpace Illegal Entry Drop ↗
    Cato / David Bier

    Updated data through February 2026 show legal entries fell about 145,000 per month versus roughly 53,000 for illegal entries—a 2.7-to-1 ratio. H-1B and H-4 issuances were down about 78 percent from February 2024 levels in Bier's analysis.

  10. 10
    ICE Surge Hits California Ahead of Midterms ↗
    Washington Examiner

    Sources described a roughly 45-day ICE enforcement surge in California ahead of the midterms, with agents directed to arrest people encountered regardless of criminal history. Local officials and advocates warned of fear in sanctuary communities as operations expanded in Southern California.
