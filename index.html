Top 10 TodayOct. 12, 2026
  1. 1
    Judge Halts Taxpayer-Funded Trump TV Ads ↗
    NBC News

    U.S. District Judge Jennifer Rochon ordered the Trump administration Sunday night to cancel remaining DHS-funded promotional TV ads, granting a temporary restraining order in a Common Cause-led suit. DHS had spent about $20 million in taxpayer funds; the White House said MAGA Inc. would fund future ads.

  2. 2
    Senators Urge Reversal of Russia Diesel Deal ↗
    Reuters

    Four Republicans and three Democrats — Collins, Murkowski, Tillis, Curtis, Shaheen, Blumenthal and Wyden — called Sunday for Trump to reverse a Russian diesel supply deal, citing the 2022 ban on Russian energy imports. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the administration will proceed under existing legal authority.

  3. 3
    Isaias Leaves 415,000-Plus Still Without Power ↗
    WJHG / Red Cross

    More than 415,000 customers across Florida, Alabama, Georgia and the Carolinas remained without power as Isaias remnants moved north. FPL said it restored nearly 200,000 of about 400,000 outages and aims for 95% restoration by Monday night; at least four storm deaths were reported.

  4. 4
    US Measles Cases Hit Highest Since 1991 ↗
    CIDRAP / CDC

    CDC reported 4,080 confirmed U.S. measles cases as of Oct. 8 — the most since 1991 — with 42 outbreaks and 95% of cases in unvaccinated people or unknown status. Pennsylvania leads with 1,136 cases; two doses of M M R vaccine are about 97% effective.

  5. 5
    Violent Threats Overwhelm Elected Officials ↗
    The New York Times

    A New York Times investigation found thousands of violent threats from 2024 through August 2026 targeting at least 44 governors, 70 senators, 185 House members and more than half of secretaries of state. Officials from both parties report fortified homes, private security and altered public schedules.

  6. 6
    NASA DOE Sign Space Nuclear Power Pact ↗
    NASA

    NASA and the Energy Department signed an M O U to accelerate space nuclear power and propulsion at the White House science summit. Goals include Space Reactor-1 Freedom in 2028 and Lunar Reactor-1 to power a future Moon base, advancing a 2030 lunar surface reactor directive.

  7. 7
    Obama Raises $13.6M for Midterm Push ↗
    CBS News

    Former President Barack Obama has raised more than $13.6 million for Democrats since last year, including $3.6 million at a D S C C fundraiser, and launched D N C ads urging voters to win what he called one of the most consequential elections in U.S. history.

  8. 8
    GOP Scrambles to Limit Steep Midterm Losses ↗
    CNN

    Republican operatives are pouring money into traditionally red states and districts amid polling that shows the House at risk and the Senate majority under threat. The Senate Leadership Fund shifted spending into Kansas and South Carolina; Cook Political Report moved 18 House races toward Democrats on Friday.

  9. 9
    Suspect Charged in Philadelphia Officer Ambush ↗
    The Philadelphia Inquirer

    Eric Lewis faces murder charges in the Saturday ambush that killed Officer Shane Seiber, 40, a father of two, and wounded his partner with 12 gunshot wounds. Police say Lewis opened fire on a West Philly porch after a gun call, then hid in a tree during a nearly hourlong standoff.

  10. 10
    US Forces Disable Ship Running Iran Blockade ↗
    Al Jazeera / CENTCOM

    U.S. Central Command said a fighter jet struck the stern of Panama-flagged bulk carrier Ocean Molica in the Gulf of Oman on Oct. 10 after it ignored warnings and tried to run the naval blockade of Iran. Propulsion was disabled; CENTCOM said the crew was not harmed.

Immigration BriefingOct. 11, 2026
  1. 1
    ICE SUV Injures Man in Houston Chase ↗
    Texas Public Radio / Houston Public Media

    On Oct. 10, an ICE-operated SUV struck Miguel Parra-Hernandez, 48, during a Clay Road pursuit near Houston; he was hospitalized with a broken leg and remains in ICE custody. ICE said he ran in front of the vehicle; Harris County deputies are investigating, and the targeted driver remained at large.

  2. 2
    DOL Freezes PERM for Microsoft and IT Giants ↗
    JD Supra / Robinson Immigration Law

    On Oct. 8, DOL announced it will not accept or process PERM green-card labor certifications for Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini. Officials alleged program misuse and cited investigations; existing H-1B status was not revoked, and no end date was announced.

  3. 3
    Coalition Sues USCIS Over Green Card Rules ↗
    SG Legal Group

    On Oct. 5, AAUP and partners sued in Massachusetts over two USCIS policies that tighten in-country green-card adjustments and allow denials without an RFE. Both policies remain in effect pending a court order; the case is AAUP v. Mullin.

  4. 4
    Admin Weighs Ships for Mass Deportations ↗
    VisaVerge (NYT-sourced)

    Officials discussed using vessels including the retired Training Ship Golden Bear and ferries for multi-day deportations to Central and South America. DHS called the idea hypothetical with no decisions made; trips could cost millions and raise safety and legal questions.

  5. 5
    BIA Limits Asylum From Developed Democracies ↗
    Law Office of Alena Shautsova

    In Matter of B-S-A-N- (Oct. 9), the Board of Immigration Appeals told judges to generally deny asylum from nationals of highly developed democracies, with a separate presumption against discretionary asylum for EU citizens, absent extraordinary circumstances.

  6. 6
    Howard County Launches ICE Activity Report Form ↗
    Howard County Government

    On Oct. 9, County Executive Calvin Ball unveiled an anonymous online form for residents and employees to report observed ICE activity, including on county property. Officials said it is for informational tracking, not emergency response.

  7. 7
    Judge Restores Biometrics for Detained Applicants ↗
    Newsweek

    On Oct. 7, Judge Amir Ali stayed a DHS policy that stopped collecting fingerprints and photos from detained people with pending USCIS applications. The order restores the prior process while the suit continues; it does not grant status or release anyone.

  8. 8
    Newsweek: ICE Detained 65 Faith Leaders ↗
    Newsweek

    A Newsweek analysis published Oct. 11 found at least 65 pastors, priests, imams, rabbis and other faith leaders detained by ICE since January 2025; 30 were released, 18 deported, and 14 remain held. DHS said enforcement targets unlawful presence, not faith.

  9. 9
    Legal Immigration Cuts Outpace Illegal Entry Drop ↗
    Cato / David Bier

    Updated data through February 2026 show legal entries fell about 145,000 per month versus roughly 53,000 for illegal entries—a 2.7-to-1 ratio. H-1B and H-4 issuances were down about 78 percent from February 2024 levels in Bier's analysis.

  10. 10
    ICE Surge Hits California Ahead of Midterms ↗
    Washington Examiner

    Sources described a roughly 45-day ICE enforcement surge in California ahead of the midterms, with agents directed to arrest people encountered regardless of criminal history. Local officials and advocates warned of fear in sanctuary communities as operations expanded in Southern California.
